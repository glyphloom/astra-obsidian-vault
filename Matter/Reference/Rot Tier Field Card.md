## When History Goes Bad

```card
id: rot-definition
q: What is rot, in Archive's vocabulary?
a: Local degradation of history that cannot settle, usually a symptom of a scar, a sheafbreak, or misused time travel.
topic: chronodaemonia/rot
created: 2026-09-09
```
Rot is what a place does when its history cannot settle: the local symptom of a scar, a sheafbreak, or somebody using time travel badly. Archive rates it on six tiers. The card below is the one taped inside every fourth-floor drawer.

| Tier | Name | What you notice | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Rot noise | Clock jitter, déjà vu, a word on the tip of every tongue | Nothing. Drink water |
| 1 | Material rot | Electronics die, isotopes drift, milk turns early, [[Errata\|errata]] in the filing | File a form |
| 2 | Section rot | Rooms rearranged, records contradicting bodies | Evacuate the floor, keep your notes |
| 3 | Entity rot | People who are almost people, versions of colleagues | Do not introduce yourself |
| 4 | Shroud rot | Folded corridors, signals that arrive corrupted | Call Diachronic Security |
| 5 | Excision | A region with no history at all; touching the edge pulls you in | Nothing. It already happened |

> [!tip] Before panicking
> Most of what feels like rot is ordinary rewriting. A rewrite re-glues your memory across your whole life, so the new past feels like it was always yours. If the world changed and you did not notice, that is working as intended. If you did notice, see [[Tomas Brandt]], and the [[Archive Glossary]] for the words.
