## Words the Fourth Floor Uses

Archive borrowed its vocabulary from the physicists and then made it sound like insurance. This is the translation I wish I had been given on day one.

> [!abstract] The short version
> History can be rebuilt. What resists rebuilding is evidence: records, witnesses, and memory. Archive sells evidence.

### Admission

Whether a thing, a person, or an event is allowed to be part of history. It has three states: yes, no (attempted, not settled), and null (not available at all).

### Scar

History that settled with a defect. It glued together well enough to last and not cleanly enough to agree. The chairman's two birthdays are a scar; see [[Project Many Happy Returns]].

### Sheafbreak

Two incompatible ways of deciding what counts as a fact, meeting and failing to agree. The centre is dangerous; the edges are calm. The [[Salt Meridian War]] is one.

### Rot

What a place does when its history cannot settle. Rated on the [[Rot Tier Field Card|six tiers]].

### Excision

A bounded region removed from history. No recoverable provenance, and the surrounding history takes the shortest way around. Legal says *incision*. See [[Clean Cut]].

### Black Provenance

Continuity without a recoverable origin. See [[Black Provenance]].

### Witness Structure

Anything that keeps a history or a person in place: ledgers, sensors, rituals, a sister who remembers your balcony. What Archive sells, and what the [[Quiet Ledger]] gives away.

### Causal Armor

Any support that makes surviving a rewrite cheaper than being deleted. It does not work inside an excision. It works outside one, which is the whole point of copying things out.

### Coherent Memory Reconstruction

After a rewrite, your memory is re-glued across your whole life, so the new past feels like it was always yours. Usually. See [[Tomas Brandt]].

### Erratum

A moth-like animal that eats records history has stopped agreeing with. Its wings carry the last text it ate. See [[Errata]].

### Rewrite Order

The order in which rewrites depend on each other. It is not calendar time; see [[Sprints Are a Theory of Time]].
