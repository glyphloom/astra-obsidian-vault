## A Field Guide to Errata

```card
id: errata-diet
q: What do errata eat?
a: Only records that history has stopped agreeing with, the superseded pages left behind after a rewrite.
topic: chronodaemonia/fauna
created: 2024-03-11
```
An erratum is a pale, moth-like animal about the size of a thumbnail. It lives wherever paper records are kept, and it eats only one thing: records that history has stopped agreeing with.

When a rewrite lands, the new past arrives everywhere at once, but the old paperwork does not update itself. Errata are why it seems to. Within hours of a rewrite they find every superseded page, ledger line, and photograph, and eat it. By morning the files agree with the world again, and nobody remembers there was ever a disagreement. They are the cleanup crew of history.

## Identification

| Feature | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Size | Thumbnail. A well-fed one reaches a stamp |
| Colour | Paper white, slightly foxed at the edges |
| Wings | Printed with fragments of the last text it ate, in the original typeface |
| Sound | A dry rustle, like someone turning a page in the next room |
| Tracks | Clean, rectangular gaps in filing |

```card
id: errata-wings
q: How can you tell what an erratum ate last?
a: Its wings carry fragments of the last text it ate, in the original typeface.
topic: chronodaemonia/fauna
created: 2024-03-11
```
The wings are the useful part. For a day or two after a meal, an erratum is the only surviving copy of what it ate. Then the print fades, like a receipt left in the sun.

## Status at Archive

Facilities lists errata as a Tier 1 infestation on the [[Rot Tier Field Card]] and sprays the basement archive every quarter. Provenance Operations quietly asks Facilities not to spray the fourth floor, because an erratum's wings are sometimes the only way to find out what a rewrite removed. Nobody has written this arrangement down, which errata would appreciate.

Errata do not eat Markdown. Nobody knows why. This is the main reason I keep a vault.

> [!warning] Handling
> Do not squash an erratum that has eaten something you need. Put it in a jar with a slice of blank paper and read it before the print fades.
