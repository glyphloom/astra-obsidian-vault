# Note granularity and atomicity

Use this review before committing a new durable note or materially enlarging one. Atomicity is a direction toward separable concerns, not a demand for small files. The test is whether a future reader could use the candidate without reopening the parent.

## Keep one note when

- It has one clear purpose: a work, source, person, project, event, topic, or argument that belongs together. Distinct durable entities, such as two companies or two places, are never one purpose, even when the owner compares them; each gets its own note with linked comparison passages.
- Its sections are necessary facets of that purpose, such as history, form, mechanism, reception, or limits.
- The apparent subtopic has no independent title, evidence base, or useful links yet.
- Splitting would duplicate the same source account, create a one-paragraph stub, or move the owner's prose away from its original context.

## Extract an atomic note when all are true

1. The concern is a concept, argument, mechanism, lineage, technique, question, or bounded entity—not merely a named example.
2. It has a concise independent title that names the concern rather than the parent or captured work.
3. It can carry a substantive account in its own right: definition/claim, why it matters, and sufficient evidence or explicitly bounded uncertainty.
4. It has at least two concrete prospective uses: separate vault connections, a likely future capture, a project application, a comparison, or a counterargument.
5. The parent can state the relationship in one sentence without losing its own coherence.

One strong question can justify a standalone article when its phrasing or cultural/technical role is itself reusable—for example, `The Hardest Question in Programming`. A merely explanatory work about a subject does not become an atom by having a title.

## Save-specific split procedure

1. Preserve the captured work and the owner's material in the work/source or existing topic note.
2. Choose the extracted note's live-vault home by role: `Infinity/` for evergreen knowledge; `Matter/` for durable references/tools; `Continuum/Media/` for media subjects; `Underwork/` for active project knowledge.
3. Put a concise inline link in the parent explaining what the child isolates. In the child, identify the parent/source where that provenance matters.
4. Give the child normal frontmatter and a full agent-owned account; do not copy the parent wholesale.
5. Create an index/MOC only when several children need a navigational surface. A parent work note is usually enough.

## Explicit-completeness requests

When the owner asks for “all,” “everything,” “every work,” or an equivalent complete set, this overrides the normal one-to-three-atom restraint. First enumerate every named or materially discussed durable item in a scope manifest. Each item must be an atomic note or have an explicit, evidence-based reason for being an incidental credit or out of scope. A mention is not a completed note.

An atom made because the owner explicitly asked for it still cannot be a stub. Give it a usable account: `### Abstract`, `### History`, and at least one subject-specific H3 section; explain its relation to the parent and two concrete consequences, connections, or future uses. If that account cannot be responsibly researched, record the limitation in the scope manifest instead of passing off a paragraph as an atom.

## Budget and restraint

For unattended saves, extract at most one to three strong atoms after the primary note is secure. If the analysis identifies a promising concern but cannot make it substantive before the commit reserve, leave a clear inline boundary in the parent and let a later related save deepen it. Do not sacrifice a coherent primary note to satisfy an abstract note-count target.
