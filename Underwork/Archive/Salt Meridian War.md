## What We Know

Between 1679 and 1688 two armies fight across the Varda salt flats. Neither is from the seventeenth century. Both are later continuations of the same 2140s, each trying to make its own version of that decade the one that happened, by winning a war three and a half centuries before it.

It is a diachronic civil war: the future fighting itself in the past. Archive owns one side's contracts, and depending on which week you ask, the other side's too.

## What Nobody Knows

- Who started it. Each side has records proving the other side attacked first, and both sets of records are genuine.
- Who is winning. The front moves when a rewrite lands, and rewrites land in dependency order, not in calendar order, so the news arrives before the battle.
- What the 2140s are actually like. Our analysts who were posted there disagree about whether it has oceans.

## Why It Is Being Cut

The flats are a sheafbreak. At the centre, two incompatible ways of deciding what counts as a fact meet and fail to agree, so bodies, maps, and memories there do not line up. The edges are calmer: the past edge keeps collecting reasons why the war was always going to happen, and the future edge keeps losing facts that cannot get through the middle.

Repairing it would take decades. Cutting it out takes one [[Clean Cut|incision]] and a very long computation. Archive chose the computation.
