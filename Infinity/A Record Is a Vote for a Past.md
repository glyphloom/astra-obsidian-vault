## Records Do Not Describe the Past, They Vote for It

When history can be rewritten, a record stops being a description and becomes a vote. Each photo, ledger line, and person who remembers is one more piece of support for one version of what happened. The version with the most support is the cheapest to keep, so it tends to be the one that stays.

That makes remembering a political act and a paid service at the same time. [[Archive]] sells votes. The [[Quiet Ledger]] gives a few away.

It also explains why the losers of a rewrite are rarely the people with the worst facts. They are the people with the fewest witnesses. [[Tomas Brandt|Tomas]] did not do anything wrong. He just had nobody writing him down.

The same logic is in statistical physics: a state that many arrangements support is the likely one. Here the arrangements are people. The companion ideas are [[Black Provenance]], for what happens when the votes are destroyed, and [[Forgetting Is a Feature]], for why nobody should want all of them counted.
